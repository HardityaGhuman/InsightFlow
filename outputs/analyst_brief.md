## Executive finding
During the period from September 2025 to January 2026, eligible signup volume grew while the overall activation rate steadily declined. This decline in activation is associated with a significant in-product drop during the onboarding process, specifically at the onboarding completion step, which represents the largest in-product loss of users before the paywall.

## Supporting evidence
*   **Signup Growth and Activation Decline:** Eligible monthly signups grew by 38.5% from 1,410 users in September 2025 to 1,953 users in January 2026. Over this same period, the activation rate fell by 8.1 percentage points, from 55.6% to 47.5%. The largest single-month decline occurred in December 2025, when the activation rate dropped by 5.9 percentage points.
*   **In-Product Funnel Loss:** The largest in-product funnel drop occurs at the "Completed onboarding" milestone, where 2,255 users were lost, representing a 68.2% pass rate from the previous stage. This is distinct from the final step to a paid subscription, which is a pricing boundary where 3,885 users did not transition to a subscription.
*   **Incomplete Recent Data:** February 2026 metrics are currently null because the 28-day measurement window has not closed for this cohort, meaning these users are not yet eligible for rate calculations.

## Most important segment signal
The strongest segment signal appears in the contrast between desktop and mobile users, which is further compounded when combined with acquisition channels. Desktop users achieved a 56.4% activation rate compared to 43.8% for mobile users, representing a 12.6 percentage point gap. 

When evaluating channel and device combinations, there is a 26.1 percentage point spread between the highest-performing segment (Referral / Desktop at 66.5% activation across 711 users) and the lowest-performing segment (Social / Mobile at 40.5% activation across 539 users). We find that some high-performing segments rest on very small cohorts, such as Referral / Tablet with only 117 users, which are highly susceptible to noise and should be interpreted cautiously.

## Recommended next investigation
We recommend investigating the onboarding flow specifically for mobile and social media signups to identify where users abandon the process. 
*   **Hypothesis 1:** The decline in overall activation rates over time is associated with the growing volume of lower-activating mobile or social signups.
*   **Hypothesis 2:** Onboarding friction is disproportionately higher on mobile devices compared to desktop.

Additionally, we suggest analyzing the transition from activation to core feature usage. Reaching the core feature within the first 7 days is associated with an 18.0% conversion rate compared to 6.8% for those who did not (an 11.2 percentage point gap). It is also associated with a 47.9% week-4 retention rate compared to 43.3% (a 4.6 percentage point gap). Note that this retention gap is relatively small compared to the conversion gap. Investigating whether early core feature adoption can be facilitated during onboarding may help clarify these relationships.

## Limitation
This analysis is based on a synthetic dataset generated with a fixed seed for a portfolio project and does not represent real customer behavior. The relationships described are associations measured on the same users; we cannot establish that completing onboarding or adopting the core feature causes higher retention or subscription conversion. Furthermore, the funnel represents milestone reach rather than a strict sequence, as 1.30% of users reached the core feature before completing onboarding.
